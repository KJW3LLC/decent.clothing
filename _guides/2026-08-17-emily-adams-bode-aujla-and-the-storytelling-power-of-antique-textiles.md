**Emily Adams Bode Aujla and the Storytelling Power of Antique Textiles**

I’ve always been fascinated by how a single piece of fabric can carry generations of stories—whether it’s a worn‑in work shirt or a delicate lace trim. When I discovered Emily Adams Bode Aujla’s label, Bode, I felt that same thrill: each garment reads like a short story stitched together from antique textiles, giving new life to forgotten histories. Let’s dive into how she turns old cloth into contemporary narrative.

## Why It Matters 🌟  
In a fashion cycle that often chases the next trend, Bode reminds us that sustainability and storytelling aren’t just buzzwords—they’re the foundation of meaningful design. By rescuing antique fabrics, Bode reduces waste while inviting wearers to consider the lives those materials once lived. It’s a refreshing counterpoint to fast fashion, showing that craft and conscience can coexist on the runway and in our closets.

## The Designer Behind the Label 👗  
Emily Adams Bode Aujla launched Bode in 2016 after years of working in costume design and textile restoration. According to the brand’s official story, she grew up surrounded by family heirlooms and developed an early fascination with the “stories embedded in cloth”【https://bode.com/pages/about】. The CFDA profile notes that her approach is rooted in “honoring the past while creating modern, wearable pieces”【https://cfda.com/members/profile/emily-adams-bode】. This blend of personal history and professional expertise shapes every collection.

## Antique Textiles as Narrative Material 🧵  
Bode’s signature move is sourcing deadstock, vintage linens, quilts, and even military uniforms—each piece chosen for its texture, patina, and history. Rather than treating these fabrics as mere raw material, she treats them as characters. A 1940s work shirt might become a modern jacket, its original stitching retained as a visible reminder of its former life. This method not only yields unique garments but also sparks conversation about provenance, labor, and the quiet dignity of everyday wear.

## Signature Collections and Techniques ✂️  
Early collections featured reworked denim jackets patched with antique lace, while later lines explored monochrome palettes built from repurposed suiting and hand‑embroidered motifs. Bode often employs visible mending, sashiko stitching, and appliqué—techniques that highlight the garment’s journey rather than hide it. The result is clothing that feels both lived‑in and intentionally crafted, inviting the wearer to add their own chapter.

## Practical Details: Designer Spotlight 🎯  
- **Background:** Founded in 2016 by Emily Adams Bode Aujla, based in New York City.  
- **Point of View:** Believes fashion should be a vessel for storytelling, sustainability, and craftsmanship.  
- **Core Craft:** Repurposing antique and deadstock textiles into contemporary menswear and womenswear, emphasizing visible mending and historical detailing.  
- **Notable Work:** Collections that blend American workwear aesthetics with delicate vintage fabrics, often showcased at New York Fashion Week.  
- **Where to Start:** Look for the “Archive” pieces on Bode’s website—these highlight the brand’s textile‑first philosophy and are perfect for anyone wanting to wear a story.

## Key Takeaways  
- Emily Adams Bode Aujla uses antique textiles as a medium for narrative, not just material.  
- Her practice merges sustainability with high design, proving that old clothes can have a futuristic appeal.  
- Visible mending and thoughtful detailing let each garment showcase its past while feeling fresh today.  
- Exploring Bode’s Archive pieces offers the clearest entry into her storytelling‑driven aesthetic.  
- Supporting designers like Bode encourages a fashion system that values history, craft, and responsible production.
