**10 Corso Como and the Store That Blended Fashion, Art, and Publishing**

When you step into 10 Corso Como, you’re not just walking into a boutique—you’re entering a living gallery where fashion, art, and publishing coexist on equal footing. This Milanese concept store has long been a pilgrimage site for those who see style as a cultural conversation rather than just a transaction.

## Why It Matters  
10 Corso Como redefined what a retail space could be by refusing to silo fashion from the creative disciplines that inspire it. By integrating exhibitions, a bookstore, and a café under one roof, it invites shoppers to linger, discover, and think about clothing as part of a broader artistic narrative【https://10corsocomo.com/pages/about-us】. This approach has influenced countless concept stores worldwide and continues to remind us that personal style thrives when it’s fed by curiosity.

## A Milanese Origin Story  
Founded in 1990 by former Vogue Italia editor Carla Sozzani, the store opened at Corso Como 10 in the Garibaldi‑Repubblica district of Milan【https://10corsocomo.com/pages/about-us】. Sozzani’s vision was to create a place where her passions for fashion, design, photography, and literature could intersect—a notion that still guides the space today.

## Fashion Meets Art  
The retail floor regularly hosts rotating art installations and design exhibitions, blurring the line between what’s for sale and what’s for contemplation. According to the store’s own narrative, the goal is to “offer a curated selection where clothing sits alongside objects that provoke thought and spark dialogue”【https://10corsocomo.com/pages/about-us】. This means you might find a striking sculptural piece next to a rack of avant‑garde jackets, encouraging you to consider how form, texture, and concept move across disciplines.

## The Publishing Edge  
A hallmark of 10 Corso Como is its dedicated bookstore, which stocks titles on fashion theory, photography, architecture, and contemporary culture. The publishing arm isn’t an afterthought; it’s presented as an essential tool for understanding the stories behind the garments on display【https://10corsocomo.com/pages/about-us】. Whether you’re looking for a limited‑edition artist monograph or a deep‑dive into textile history, the shelves invite you to extend your shopping trip into a mini‑research session.

## What to Expect Today  
- **Location:** Corso Como 10, Milan, Italy (Garibaldi‑Repubblica area)【https://10corsocomo.com/pages/about-us】  
- **Offering:** A mix of menswear, womenswear, accessories, design objects, art books, and a café that serves light bites and espresso.  
- **Who it’s for:** Anyone who views shopping as an opportunity to engage with culture—from fashion students and designers to curious travelers and locals seeking inspiration.  
- **What makes it distinct:** The seamless integration of retail, exhibition space, and publishing creates a environment where you can browse a newly released photo book, sip coffee amid a sculpture, and try on a jacket that echoes the same aesthetic themes—all without leaving the same floor.

> **💡 Pro Tip:** If you’re short on time, start at the bookstore on the mezzanine level; the staff often highlight titles that connect directly to the current fashion installations, giving you a quick cultural primer before you hit the racks.

> **⚠️ Watch Out:** Because the store’s exhibitions change frequently, it’s worth checking the online calendar before you go to see what’s on view—this helps you align your visit with the themes that interest you most.

> **🎯 Key Insight:** 10 Corso Como shows that a store can be a cultural hub first and a retailer second; when the environment feels like a gallery, the clothes you find there tend to feel more like extensions of an artistic conversation rather than mere commodities.

- Founded by Carla Sozzani in 1990, it pioneered the modern concept‑store model.  
- The space hosts rotating exhibitions, a curated bookstore, and a café, encouraging a multidisciplinary shopping experience.
